Military Information

An encyclopedic reference on military doctrine and organization

Army — Ground Forces

Ground forces form the foundation of any nation's military power. The army encompasses all land-based combat and support units, from armored formations to logistics. Modern armies rely on combined arms doctrine — the coordinated use of infantry, armor, artillery, and support — to achieve tactical objectives.

Main Battle Tanks

The decisive instrument of armored warfare. MBTs combine firepower, protection, and mobility to break through enemy lines and exploit breakthroughs.

Artillery & SPG

Long-range fire support systems that shape the battlefield before direct engagement. Self-propelled guns provide mobile fire support to advancing forces.

APC & IFV

Armored vehicles that transport infantry under fire and provide direct fire support. Essential for combined arms operations in contested environments.

Support Vehicles

The backbone of battlefield logistics — fuel, ammunition, maintenance, and command vehicles that keep combat forces operational.

Air Force — Aerial Power

Air superiority is the prerequisite for all other military operations. A nation without control of its airspace cannot effectively project ground or naval power. Modern air forces integrate fighters, bombers, electronic warfare, and support aircraft into a unified system.

Fighters

Multi-role aircraft designed for air-to-air combat and ground attack. The primary instrument of air superiority operations.

Interceptors

High-speed aircraft optimized for rapid response to aerial threats. Designed to destroy enemy bombers and reconnaissance aircraft.

Strategic Bombers

Long-range aircraft capable of delivering conventional or strategic payloads deep into enemy territory.

Special Mission

Electronic warfare, reconnaissance, and airborne early warning aircraft that multiply the effectiveness of combat units.

Navy — Maritime Power

Naval power determines a nation's ability to project force globally, protect sea lanes, and deny the enemy use of maritime domains. The modern navy encompasses surface combatants, submarines, carrier aviation, and amphibious forces.

Aircraft Carriers

The pinnacle of naval power projection. Carriers bring airpower to any ocean and serve as mobile airbases for strike and defense operations.

Destroyers & Frigates

The workhorses of the modern fleet. These versatile surface combatants conduct anti-air, anti-submarine, and anti-surface warfare.

Submarines

Stealth instruments of maritime warfare. Submarines conduct reconnaissance, strike operations, and sea denial missions with strategic impact.

Battleships & Cruisers

Heavy surface combatants providing overwhelming firepower. Capital ships that anchor naval formations and project power ashore.

Military Doctrine

Doctrine defines how a military organization uses its forces to achieve strategic objectives. The dominant doctrines in modern warfare emphasize speed, jointness, and information advantage over mass and attrition.

Combined arms warfare — the synchronized use of infantry, armor, aviation, and fires — has proven decisive since World War II. Network-centric warfare adds information dominance as a force multiplier. Maneuver warfare prioritizes disrupting enemy decision-making over territorial control.

Logistics & Sustainment

No military campaign survives without logistics. The sustained delivery of fuel, ammunition, food, medical support, and spare parts determines how long a force can remain effective in the field. Modern militaries measure combat power not just in units, but in days of supply — the duration a force can sustain operations before resupply becomes critical.

Support vehicles, maintenance vessels, and transport aircraft form the unglamorous but decisive backbone of any sustained campaign. A numerically superior force with weak logistics will lose to a smaller, well-supplied opponent over time.

Command Structure

Military command follows a hierarchical chain from strategic to tactical levels. At the strategic level, national leadership sets overall war aims. The operational level translates strategy into campaigns across theaters. The tactical level executes specific engagements on the battlefield.

Joint command — the coordination of army, air force, and navy under unified leadership — is the hallmark of modern military organization. Nations that fail to integrate their branches suffer from duplicated effort and missed opportunities for combined arms effect.

Equipment Classification

Military hardware is classified by role, weight, and strategic function rather than simply by type. Light equipment (light tanks, corvettes, light bombers) trades protection and payload for speed and numbers. Heavy equipment (MBTs, battleships, heavy bombers) sacrifices mobility for decisive firepower and survivability.

Special mission platforms — electronic warfare aircraft, minesweepers, maintenance vessels — rarely engage directly but multiply the effectiveness of front-line combat units through support, detection, and sustainment.

Rank & Score Interpretation

A nation's position in the HMR ranking reflects the composite strength of its declared military assets, not a prediction of outcome in any specific conflict. Rankings shift instantly as administrators update military data, reflecting the dynamic nature of military development.

A high overall rank does not guarantee dominance in every domain — a nation may lead in naval power while ranking lower in air capability. The branch-specific rankings (Army, Air Force, Navy) exist precisely to reveal this nuance.

Strategic Concepts

Power projection refers to a nation's ability to deploy and sustain military force beyond its own borders — carriers, strategic bombers, and blue-water navies are the primary instruments. Deterrence is the use of demonstrated capability to prevent conflict before it begins, relying on an opponent's rational calculation of unacceptable cost.

Area denial and anti-access strategies use submarines, coastal defense, and layered air defense to prevent an adversary from operating freely in contested space — a cost-effective counter to raw numerical superiority.

The HMR Index — Ranking Methodology

The Havain Military Rank Index is a composite score calculated from all available military data. Each branch receives a weighted score based on unit counts and strategic value of individual systems. Naval capital ships, carrier aviation, and strategic bombers receive the highest weights.

Manpower multipliers account for active and reserve personnel. Economic power (GDP) modifies the raw equipment score to reflect industrial capacity and sustainability in prolonged conflict. The final HMR Index represents an overall assessment of military potential across all domains.
